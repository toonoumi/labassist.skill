# B&W paper (print) developers

Print development is fundamentally different from film development: you
develop **to completion**, not for a calculated time tied to exposure.
Enlarger exposure (time/aperture at the enlarging stage) controls density;
development time mostly just needs to hit "fully developed" and stay there
— that's what makes tray development forgiving. Cutting development short
(under ~45 sec for most modern developers) causes uneven, muddy blacks and
should be avoided; going a bit long rarely hurts.

All times are at **20°C (68°F)**, continuous gentle tray rocking agitation
throughout. As with `dev-times.md`, treat these as commonly published
starting points, not gospel — confirm against the manufacturer's datasheet
for anything going into an important print.

## Common print developers

| Developer | Dilution | RC paper time | Fiber (FB) paper time | Tone |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kodak Dektol | 1+2 | 60-90 sec | 90 sec-2:00 | Neutral-cold, the classic default |
| Kodak Dektol | 1+3 | 90 sec | 2:00 | Slightly softer, more open shadows |
| Ilford Multigrade Developer | 1+9 | 60 sec | 90 sec | Neutral |
| Ilford Multigrade Developer | 1+14 | 90 sec | 2:00 | Softer, better highlight separation |
| Ilford PQ Universal | 1+9 | 60 sec | 90 sec | Neutral; also usable for film |
| Kodak Selectol | 1+1 | 90 sec | 2:00-3:00 | Warm tone |
| Kodak Selectol-Soft | 1+1 | 90 sec | 2:00-3:00 | Warm, lower contrast — often blended with Dektol to fine-tune contrast/tone |
| Ethol LPD | 1+3 | 60-90 sec | 90 sec-2:00 | Cold-neutral, popular commercial standard |
| Ethol LPD | 1+4 to 1+5 (approx) | 90 sec-2:00 | 2:00-3:00 | Warmer as dilution increases |
| Ansco 130 (approx) | Stock | 60-90 sec | 90 sec-2:00 | Warm-neutral, classic formula |

## Full print process (typical, RC or fiber as noted)

| Step | Chemical | Dilution | Time | Agitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Develop | (chosen developer) | (chosen dilution) | (from table above) | Continuous tray rocking |
| Stop bath | Indicator stop bath (or plain water) | 1+19 | 10-15 sec | Continuous |
| Fix | Rapid fixer, paper strength (more dilute than film strength) | 1+9 | 60 sec (RC) / 3-5 min, two-bath (fiber) | Continuous |
| Wash | Running water | — | 4 min (RC) | — |
| Wash | Running water, or archival washer + hypo clearing agent | — | 20-60 min (fiber) | — |
| Dry | — | — | — | RC: air dry face-up on a screen. Fiber: screen or blotters, may need flattening under weight afterward |

## Notes specific to paper

- **Two-bath fixing** is best practice for both RC and fiber (30 sec-1 min
  per bath for RC, 2-3 min per bath for fiber) rather than one longer
  single bath — extends usable fixer capacity and improves archival
  permanence.
- **Fiber-base paper** holds onto fixer and wash byproducts much more
  stubbornly than RC. A hypo clearing agent (HCA) bath between fix and
  wash is what makes a practical (rather than hour-plus) fiber wash time
  possible — don't skip it for anything meant to last.
- **No push/pull, no reel/tank agitation math** — those concepts are
  film-specific. If a user's question is actually about printing (dodging,
  burning, contrast filtration, exposure), that's outside this skill's
  scope; only the developer/stop/fix/wash chemistry table applies here.
