# Full process (beyond the developer step)

Present these steps alongside the developer step so the table is a complete
guide, not just a dev time.

| Step | Chemical | Dilution | Time | Temp | Agitation |
|------|----------|----------|------|------|-----------|
| Pre-rinse (optional) | Water | — | 1:00 | 20°C | Continuous |
| Develop | (per user's chosen developer) | (per user's choice) | (calculated) | 20°C | See agitation.md |
| Stop bath | Stop bath (or plain water) | 1+9 if using indicator stop | 0:30-1:00 | 20°C | Continuous |
| Fix | Rapid fixer | 1+4 (film strength) | 4:00-8:00* | 20°C | 30s continuous, then 5-10s/30-60s |
| Wash | Running water, or Ilford method** | — | 5:00-10:00 | 18-24°C | — |
| Final rinse | Photo-Flo / wetting agent | 1+200 | 0:30 | 20°C | No agitation, dunk once |
| Dry | — | — | — | — | Hang in dust-free area, weighted bottom |

\* Fix time depends on the specific fixer and film — 4-6 min for most rapid
fixers on modern films, up to 8 min for older/thicker emulsions. If unknown,
recommend the "clip test" (drop a film leader snip in fixer, time until it
clears, then fix for 2x that time).

\*\* Ilford wash method: fill tank with water, invert 5x, dump; refill,
invert 10x, dump; refill, invert 20x, dump. Equivalent to a long running
wash and saves water.

## Time-temperature compensation

If the user's water/chemistry is not at 20°C, adjust the calculated
developer time — roughly **±20% time per ±3°C (5°F)** from 20°C, within a
sane working range of about 18-24°C. Outside that range, recommend they
bring the chemistry to temperature rather than compensating with time alone
— extreme temperatures distort contrast even if density comes out right.

Always mention: keep developer, stop, and fix all within a couple degrees of
each other to avoid reticulation (grain-damaging temperature shock).
